Embrace the experience

Whatever you picture, Cambodia has a version of it.

Temples & history

Angkor Wat, Bayon and hundreds of forest temples, from Khmer Empire grandeur to modern memorials.

Islands & coast

Powder beaches on Koh Rong, bioluminescent plankton at night and slow days in Kep.

Food & markets

Fish amok, beef lok lak, noodle soups and Kampot pepper crab, best eaten at the market.

Nature & wildlife

Irrawaddy dolphins at Kratié, waterfalls in Ratanakiri and elephant sanctuaries in Mondulkiri.

Khmer food

Gentle, fresh and fragrant, built on lemongrass, galangal, kroeung paste and river fish.

FAQs

Do I need US dollars or riel?

Both work. US dollars are used for most prices and ATMs dispense them. Riel (about 4,000 per US$1) is used for change under a dollar. Bring crisp notes: torn or marked bills are often refused.

Is Cambodia safe for tourists?

Most visits are trouble-free. Watch for bag snatching from tuk-tuks in Phnom Penh, keep a copy of your passport, and take out travel insurance. Police 117, ambulance 119, Tourist Police 012 942 484.

How many days do I need?

Plan 3 days for Siem Reap and Angkor, 2 for Phnom Penh, and 3 to 4 more for the coast or the countryside. Ten to fourteen days covers a good loop.

Can I use my phone?

Yes. Local SIMs from Cellcard, Smart or Metfone cost a few dollars for several GB and are sold at the airports, or use an eSIM bought before you fly.

What should I wear at temples?

Cover shoulders and knees at Angkor and at pagodas, and remove shoes and hats before entering shrines.
